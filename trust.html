Trust, evidence & boundaries

Security claims are only useful when the system can show its work.

ComplianceFlow is designed around a simple distinction: an observation is not the same thing as a conclusion. Cloud evidence is collected first, relationships are derived deterministically, remediation is controlled, and security-effect claims are made only after fresh evidence supports them.

Evidence firstProvider observations remain tied to their source and observation time.
Uncertainty stays visiblePotential or inconclusive states are not promoted to verified results.
AI explains; it does not decideThe analyst can explain deterministic state but cannot manufacture evidence or override it.

The trust model

These are the boundaries that matter when a compliance system is allowed to inspect infrastructure and recommend or execute changes.

01 / AUTHORITY

Durable state lives in PostgreSQL

Execution state, findings, evidence, exposure paths and audit events use PostgreSQL as the durable authority. Redis/BullMQ is used for dispatch and work delivery, not as security truth.

02 / OBSERVATION

Relationships keep their provenance

Cloud relationships and exposure paths are tied back to provider observations. A correlated relationship without sufficient evidence remains inferred or potential rather than verified.

03 / REMEDIATION

Changing a resource is not proof of fixing risk

An authorized cloud operation moves through a verification lifecycle. The system collects fresh provider evidence after the change instead of treating an API success response as proof.

04 / REANALYSIS

Security effect requires a fresh comparison

Path and risk reduction claims require distinct baseline and fresh scans, independent evidence lineage, and deterministic reanalysis. Incomplete analysis cannot produce a safe reduction claim.

05 / MULTI-CLOUD

One model across five providers

ComplianceFlow's provider layer supports AWS, Azure, GCP, DigitalOcean and Hetzner. Provider-specific observations feed the same evidence, risk and remediation boundaries.

06 / AI

The model is downstream of evidence

The AI analyst receives grounded findings, graph state and proof state. It can explain severity, uncertainty and remediation context, but it cannot invent relationships or infer compromise from correlation.

What a result actually means

ComplianceFlow deliberately uses different words for different levels of certainty.

Observed

A provider-backed observation exists with identifiable evidence lineage.

Verified

The observation satisfies the deterministic verification requirements for that state.

Potential

A relationship or exposure is plausible, but the evidence is not sufficient to present it as verified.

Inconclusive

The available evidence cannot safely establish the requested state. Uncertainty remains uncertainty.

Remediated

An authorized remediation was applied and passed the relevant verification lifecycle.

Proven reduction

Independent fresh evidence and deterministic reanalysis show measurable security improvement.

Claims we intentionally do not make

  • Correlation between resources does not prove compromise.
  • An AI-generated explanation does not override provider evidence or deterministic proof state.
  • A successful remediation API call does not, by itself, prove the cloud state changed.
  • A missing, stale or incomplete rescan cannot prove that an exposure path disappeared.
  • A “verified” control is not automatically a claim that every related risk is eliminated.
